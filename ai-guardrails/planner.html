AI Guardrails

Plan your Business Central permissions in two minutes

Seven questions about how your company actually works. You'll get a recommended setup and a blueprint code that AI Guardrails reads, so its setup skips straight to your plan.

Question 1 of 7
How many people sign in to Business Central?

This decides how many separate roles are worth keeping up.

When someone is out for a week, what happens to their work?

If coworkers routinely fill in, very narrow roles will get in their way.

What does your company mostly do in Business Central?

This picks the starter roles that get loaded.

A vendor emails to say their bank account changed. Who should be able to update it?

Fake bank-change emails are one of the most common ways companies lose money.

Someone raises a customer's credit limit without asking. What should happen?

This covers key fields like credit limits, vendor bank details and prices.

Does anyone outside the company review your controls?

An auditor, lender, insurer or parent company, for example.

Should some people only see their own location or company?

For example, a warehouse lead who should only see their own warehouse.

    The three setups

    Top-down tiers suit small teams where people cover for each other: a few broad roles with firm guardrails.

    Role-based suits clear jobs: each person gets only the abilities their job needs.

    Hybrid sits in between: a tier sets how far each person can reach, and abilities set what their job needs.

    Read Permissions 101 for the full picture.

    You can change anything later

    The blueprint only picks a starting point. Every role, ability and protection can be edited in Business Central afterwards, and nothing changes for your users until you go live. Everything can be rolled back.
