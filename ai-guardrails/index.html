Six steps from wide-open to locked down

The app opens on a checklist and walks you through these in order. Nothing changes for your users until step 5.

See where you stand

A read-only scan of every user: SUPER users, disabled users who still have access, people with no role, and access outside their role.

Answer a few questions

Plain-language questions about your company pick the setup that fits: simple tiers, job-based roles, or a mix. Or answer them here first with the Permissions Planner.

Review your people

Each person gets a suggested role based on what they actually posted in the last 90 days, with the reason shown. Adjust anything.

Try it safely

Check that each person can still do their everyday tasks, then try the new roles for a day. Access goes back automatically unless you keep it.

Go live

Apply roles one department at a time. Current access is saved first, and Roll Back puts it back exactly.

Stay safe

Turn on monitoring, alerts and protected fields so access doesn't drift back to where it was.

What's in it

One role per person

A role is a tier (how far up someone can reach) plus abilities (what their job needs). Abilities are built on Microsoft's standard permission sets, so updates keep working. Permissions 101 explains it in three minutes.

Guardrails around the risky areas

Security and users, G/L and finance setup, bank accounts, vendor and customer bank details, and HR stay away from people who don't need them.

Protected fields

Pick any field, like vendor bank details, credit limits or prices, and either log or block changes by anyone not allowed. It works at the database level, so it holds on every page, API, import and Edit in Excel.

Temporary access that removes itself

Someone needs extra access for an afternoon? They request it with a reason, someone else approves, and it's removed automatically when time's up.

Access Help for blocked users

Paste the "You do not have the following permissions" error and see which ability grants it, then request temporary access in one click.

Access reviews with sign-off

Each person's access goes to their reviewer to confirm, remove or change. Sign-off needs a decision on every line.

Who can do what, exactly

See who can read, insert, change or delete any table, from Business Central's own effective permissions.

License savings

People on Essentials or Premium who only need to look things up are flagged as Team Member candidates.

Security score and alerts

A 0 to 100 score on the setup page, regular re-scans, and alerts on new high-risk findings by email or to a Teams channel.

Reports auditors ask for

User access, change log, review sign-off, security scan and temporary access, as Excel reports. Auditors get their own read-only permission set.

Why it's called AI Guardrails

If you're connecting an AI agent to Business Central, its access is a permission problem like any other. AI Guardrails gives an agent a role with only the abilities it needs, and its read-only APIs let a security agent watch roles, scan findings, temporary access and the audit log without holding admin rights itself.

It's also what the AI for Business Central setup uses to keep AI access in check.
