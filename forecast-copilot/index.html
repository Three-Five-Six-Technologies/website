Review the items that need a decision, not every item.

Every night, Forecast Copilot builds a baseline forecast for each item in Business Central and flags only the ones that need your forecaster: stockout risk, excess stock, bias and sudden changes in demand. Publish writes to BC's standard Demand Forecast, so planning keeps working the way it does today.

A forecast cycle, start to finish

The forecaster works from a short list of exceptions instead of a spreadsheet with every item in it.

A baseline every night

A job queue entry builds monthly sales history for each item and forecasts the months ahead, adjusted for seasons once there's a year of history.

Only what needs you

Items are flagged for stockout risk, excess, bias, demand changes, new items, and overrides that did worse than the baseline.

Decide in the workbench

Accept the suggestion, keep the baseline, or override with a reason. Then jump to the next exception.

Publish to BC

Final numbers go to the standard Demand Forecast, so MRP and planning worksheets use them. A snapshot is saved for later.

See how it went

Each month, the published forecast and the baseline are both measured against what actually sold.

What's in it

Six kinds of exceptions

Stockout risk, excess, bias, demand signal, new item, and overrides that are hurting accuracy. Reviewed items stay quiet for 30 days so the list doesn't fill with repeats.

Forecast Workbench

One page per item with sales history, last year, open sales orders, days of supply and a "why this suggestion" panel. Overrides need a reason code, so you know later why the number changed.

From stockout risk to a purchase line

A stockout risk shows the projected stockout date. One click adds a requisition line with the suggested quantity and the item's vendor.

Accuracy you can trust

Monthly accuracy and bias for the published forecast and for the baseline, so you can see whether overrides are adding value or costing it.

On the Item Card

A Forecast Insight panel on the item card and item list shows the next three months, days of supply and any open exception.

For people outside BC

Sales and managers can see the inbox, item detail and accuracy in the client portal, read-only, with the same Microsoft sign-in. Read-only APIs feed Power BI or Excel too.

Built for a Forecaster role

Forecast Copilot adds a Forecaster role center with a tile for each kind of exception and an accuracy tile, so the day starts with what changed overnight. It uses BC's own items, item ledger entries, demand forecasts and requisition worksheets. Nothing new for purchasing to learn.

Where it is today

Version 0.1, with 80 automated tests passing in a Business Central sandbox. Not built yet:

  • Weekly buckets, and forecasting by variant or across several locations at once
  • Using retail sell-through from TFS EDI 852s as a demand signal
  • Editing or publishing the forecast from the portal
