Everyone gets one role

In standard Business Central, people collect permission sets over the years until nobody knows why anyone can do anything. AI Guardrails starts from a simpler rule: each person gets exactly one role. If you can say what someone's role is, you can say what they can do.

A role is a tier plus abilities

Abilities are what a job needs: post sales invoices, receive purchase orders, run inventory adjustments. Each ability is a small permission set built on Microsoft's standard D365 sets, so Business Central updates keep working. You edit a role by ticking abilities on and off.

The tier is how far up someone can reach: ADMIN, MANAGER, STAFF, CLERK or VIEW. A tier doesn't add anything. It takes guardrails away.

Guardrails protect the places fraud and expensive mistakes happen

Security and users, G/L and finance setup, bank accounts, vendor and customer bank details, and HR. A clerk-tier role loses all of these even if an ability would otherwise include them. If one person really does need one, say a named person who maintains vendor bank details, you add that guardrail to their role on purpose.

Three ways to organize it

  • Top-down tiers for small teams where people cover for each other. A few broad roles with firm guardrails.
  • Role-based modules for clear jobs. Each person gets only the abilities their job needs.
  • Hybrid in between. A tier sets how far each person can reach; abilities set what their job needs.

Not sure which fits? The Permissions Planner asks seven questions and tells you.

Changing access without breaking anyone's day

Before anything changes, AI Guardrails checks each person's everyday tasks against their new role. You can then try roles for a day: access is applied and put back automatically unless you keep it. When you go live, current access is saved first, and Roll Back restores exactly what people had before.

After go-live

Protected fields log or block changes to things like credit limits and vendor bank details on every page, API and Excel edit. Temporary access covers the one-off afternoon someone needs more, and removes itself. Regular scans and alerts catch access drifting back to where it was.
